If you last played 40K a few years ago, the hard part is not learning new rules — it is filtering noise. Most coverage focuses on reveals and hype cycles. What matters for returning players is simpler: list-building friction, game speed, and whether your existing collection still plays the way you remember.

## The short version

- Core gameplay is still recognizably 40K: movement discipline, target priority, and trading units still decide most games.
- The biggest practical shift is **how quickly list assumptions expire** as points, detachments, and mission expectations move.
- Returning players get the best results by upgrading tools first (cards, tokens, terrain habits), then models.

## What changed in practice (not in marketing)

### 1) Index comfort is gone faster than before
Early-edition comfort lists now age out quickly. If your muscle memory was built on one static codex window, you will feel this immediately.

**Trade-off:** faster updates can improve balance, but they also punish players who only touch the game every few months.

### 2) Mission play is less optional
Older casual groups could often win by pure kill pressure. Current tables reward scoring discipline much more consistently.

What this means for returning players:
- Budget CP and resources for secondaries/positioning turns, not just damage spikes.
- Bring units that can *do jobs* (actions, screening, objective flips), not only premium damage dealers.

### 3) Detachment identity matters more than faction nostalgia
A faction you love may still be good — just not in the style you remember. The detachment layer is where many armies now gain their real play pattern.

**Caveat:** this can feel like your old army got "rewritten" even if the datasheets look familiar.

## What to buy first (and what to delay)

### Buy first
1. **Play aids over plastic**: objective markers, wound trackers, printed mission pack references.  
2. **One flexible utility unit** for your army (screening / action / scoring role).  
3. **Terrain-friendly transport and storage** so you can play more often with less setup friction.

### Delay
- Niche anti-meta tech pieces you saw in one event-winning list.
- Expensive centerpiece kits before 4–6 games of reps.
- Full re-army rebuilds based on one update cycle.

## Common returning-player mistakes

- Chasing tournament lists before relearning your local meta.
- Overbuilding damage and underbuilding board control.
- Assuming old "auto-includes" are still efficient after updates.

A better approach: run a 1,500–2,000 point "re-entry" list with one experimental slot each game. Track what underperforms in your own reps.

## Who should skip upgrading right now

You should probably wait 4–8 weeks if:
- You only play once every couple of months and dislike update churn.
- Your group is moving to a different system (Kill Team, Old World, AoS) for the season.
- Your current army requires multiple premium kits to become functional in your preferred style.

Waiting is not quitting. It is often the better EV move if your hobby budget is tight.

## Better re-entry plan (low regret)

Week 1–2:
- Play two games with mostly existing models.
- Capture where games were lost: scoring, mobility, durability, or output.

Week 3–4:
- Buy one unit that solves your top failure mode.
- Re-test on the same mission format.

Week 5+:
- Only then commit to bigger purchases.

This sequence protects budget and gets you table reps faster than theory-crafting from social feeds.

## Final take

11th-edition-era 40K is still rewarding for returning players, but it rewards **process** more than impulse purchases. If you treat the first month as a calibration phase, you will spend less, relearn faster, and avoid rebuilding your army twice.
