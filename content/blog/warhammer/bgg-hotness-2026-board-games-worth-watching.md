If you only have time to track one trend list each week, BGG Hotness is still the fastest signal for what people are actually clicking, discussing, and pre-ordering.

This week’s standouts lean heavily toward new 2026 titles and medium-to-heavy strategy. That is good for excitement, but it also means higher rules overhead and a bigger risk of buying something your regular group won’t table twice.

## Quick picks first

- **Most likely to land with strategy groups:** *The Queen’s Dilemma*
- **Best table-presence swing:** *First Giants*
- **Most likely to bounce with casual groups:** *World Order* (theme + complexity risk)
- **Safest “wait for reviews” pick:** *Shards of Creation*

## 1) Possess Me, Satan

Why it is hot: strong novelty pull and social buzz.

Likely fit:
- groups that enjoy tense negotiation and dramatic turns
- players happy with conflict at the table

Trade-off / caveat:
- social pressure mechanics can be exhausting after work
- if your group dislikes “targeting” dynamics, this can feel mean rather than fun

Who should skip:
- anyone wanting a low-friction, teach-in-10-minutes game night

## 2) The Queen’s Dilemma

Why it is hot: narrative strategy crowd is treating this as a “watch closely” release.

Likely fit:
- recurring groups that like campaign continuity
- players who enjoy debating outcomes more than optimising every move

Trade-off / caveat:
- campaign formats demand attendance consistency
- if two players miss sessions often, the narrative payoff drops quickly

Who should skip:
- mixed groups with irregular schedules

## 3) World Order

Why it is hot: big-theme strategy with table talk potential.

Likely fit:
- experienced groups already comfortable with heavier euro/political systems

Trade-off / caveat:
- the teach can be the whole first night
- downtime risk if your group has AP-prone players

Who should skip:
- anyone still building confidence beyond gateway strategy titles

## 4) Lands of Evershade

Why it is hot: strong art direction plus early interest from engine-building fans.

Likely fit:
- players who enjoy long-form planning and tableau development

Trade-off / caveat:
- pretty production often hides a punishing first play
- if iconography is dense, onboarding can be rough without a rules explainer video

Who should skip:
- groups that prefer immediate tactical play over setup/planning arcs

## 5) Shards of Creation

Why it is hot: broad curiosity and “could be huge” discourse.

Likely fit:
- hobby gamers willing to learn one complex system deeply

Trade-off / caveat:
- high upside, high risk until full post-release consensus lands
- balancing and pacing questions are still open

Who should skip:
- value-first buyers who only purchase after six-month sentiment stabilises

## 6) First Giants

Why it is hot: visual hook + big-box ambition.

Likely fit:
- groups that like eventful sessions and dramatic board states

Trade-off / caveat:
- table space and setup time may be a practical blocker
- “epic” can mean fewer plays per month

Who should skip:
- smaller households and groups that need 60–90 minute games

## 7) Morty Sorty Magic Shop

Why it is hot: lighter tone in a mostly heavy-looking week.

Likely fit:
- families and mixed-experience tables looking for a playful theme

Trade-off / caveat:
- lighter systems can have lower long-term depth for dedicated strategy groups

Who should skip:
- groups seeking crunchy optimisation and long planning horizons

## 8) Let’s Go! To France

Why it is hot: approachable concept and broad accessibility.

Likely fit:
- couples and casual groups needing low setup friction

Trade-off / caveat:
- accessible often means lower tension for hardcore gamers

Who should skip:
- tables expecting strong competitive bite

## 9) Nippon: Zaibatsu

Why it is hot: known brand power with returning interest.

Likely fit:
- gamers who already know they enjoy heavier economic design

Trade-off / caveat:
- complexity can punish rusty groups; first play may feel procedural

Who should skip:
- anyone who wants an intuitive first-night experience

## Buy smarter this week: use a two-step filter

Before pre-ordering any Hotness mover, ask:

1. **Will this hit the table three times in eight weeks?**
2. **Do we have the right group for its teach + downtime profile?**

If either answer is “not sure”, wait for full-playthrough coverage and first-wave player reports.

That single pause saves more money than chasing every trending release.
