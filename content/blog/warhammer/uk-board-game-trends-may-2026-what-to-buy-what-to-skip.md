If you only buy one trend-led game this month, make it a game that already has stable stock at at least two UK retailers. Hype charts are useful, but stock gaps and inflated resale pricing can turn a “smart buy” into a regret buy in about 48 hours.

## What’s actually trending right now

From this week’s trend scan:
- BoardGameGeek attention is still clustering around crossover titles, with **Terraria: The Board Game** pulling visibility.
- Retail chatter points to a late-May retail window for Terraria in some stores.
- Crowdfunding interest remains high, but delivery risk and VAT/shipping variance are still where most buyers get burned.

From internal Supabase history (recent article velocity), “timely analysis + practical buyer filter” formats are outperforming pure announcement posts, which is why this guide is blunt by design.

## Trend #1: Big IP board games are still the safest short-term bet

Why it’s working:
- Built-in audience reduces rules teach friction at normal mixed tables.
- Easier resale if your group bounces off it.
- More likely to get post-launch support and community rules clarifications.

Trade-off / caveat:
- You often pay an IP premium for minis/art rather than better scenario design.
- Some IP games look great, then stall after 2–3 plays because mission variety is shallow.

Who should skip:
- Skip if your group prioritises high replay depth over theme.
- Skip if you already have a campaign backlog and finish fewer than one campaign every two months.

## Trend #2: Crowdfunding remains attractive, but cashflow risk is real

Why people still back:
- Better component value at pledge stage than post-retail bundles.
- Stretch-goal bundles can be strong if you know your group will actually play the content.

Trade-off / caveat:
- Delivery timelines are still slippery; “Q4 delivery” can become “next spring” quickly.
- UK landed cost can swing hard once shipping and VAT settle.

Who should skip:
- Skip if you want guaranteed table time in the next 8 weeks.
- Skip if your gaming budget is fixed monthly and can’t absorb late shipping invoices.

## Trend #3: “Hotness” buying is useful only with a table-fit filter

A practical table-fit check before buying:
- **Player count reality:** does your group reliably hit the game’s best count?
- **Teach load:** can someone explain it cleanly in under 15 minutes?
- **Session length:** are you actually finishing 2+ hour sessions right now?

Trade-off / caveat:
- The hottest games are often anticipation-heavy (pre-release or just landed), so reviews can lag behind hype.

Who should skip:
- Skip hotness-led buys if your group is currently requesting lighter 45–90 minute games.

## Quick buy framework (under 5 minutes)

Use this before checkout:
1. Confirm two UK stock options (avoid panic pricing).
2. Check one critical review mentioning downside, not just praise.
3. Write one sentence: “This replaces X in our rotation because Y.”
4. If you can’t write #3, don’t buy yet.

## Bottom line

May 2026 trends are real, but the winners are buyers who filter hype through table fit and stock reality. If your group wants immediate play, prioritise in-stock retail buys. If you back crowdfunding, treat delivery timing as uncertain by default.

That sounds less exciting, but it’s how you avoid shelf queens and buyer’s remorse.
