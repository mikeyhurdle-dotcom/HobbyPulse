If you only buy one game from this week's BGG Hotness list, make it **First Giants**.

Not because it's automatically "better" than everything else, but because it's the easiest recommendation for most groups right now: broad table appeal, lighter teach, and fewer ways to bounce off in game one.

Below is the short version for the top movers we keep seeing in player discussions: **First Giants**, **The Legacy of Mars**, **Dungeons of the Oak Dell**, and **The Lord of the Rings: Fate of the Fellowship**.

## Quick verdict (UK buyer view)

- **Best all-round pick right now:** First Giants
- **Best for heavier planners:** The Legacy of Mars
- **Best if your group likes tactical combat systems:** Dungeons of the Oak Dell
- **Best for LOTR fans who prioritise theme over speed:** Fate of the Fellowship

## 1) First Giants (2026)

### Best for
- Groups that want a new strategy game without a brutal first teach
- Mixed-experience tables (one hobby gamer + casual players)
- Players who care about replay value from session two onward

### Skip if
- Your group only enjoys high-conflict, take-that-heavy games
- You want very deep engine chaining from turn one

### The trade-off
**First Giants trades raw complexity for smoother table fit.** That's a strength for most groups, but heavier gamers may find the decision space less punishing than they want.

### Why it beats the alternatives for most buyers
Compared with other hotness movers this week, it gives you the cleanest route to getting it played repeatedly instead of admired on a shelf. If your buying goal is "most likely to hit the table," this is the low-risk pick.

## 2) The Legacy of Mars (2027)

### Best for
- Players who already enjoy long-horizon planning
- Groups who like terraforming/economy-style optimisation puzzles
- Tables willing to accept a slower first game for higher long-term payoff

### Skip if
- Your game night is usually under 90 minutes
- You have players who tune out during dense rules explanation

### The trade-off
**You get strategic depth, but you pay in teach friction and pace.** If your group doesn't like front-loaded rules overhead, this can feel like work before it feels fun.

### Hidden drawback most previews miss
At mixed groups, the experience can widen: one player "gets" the economy arc quickly while others are still learning timing windows. That gap can make first impressions worse than the design deserves.

## 3) Dungeons of the Oak Dell (2025)

### Best for
- Players who want tactical combat with meaningful hit-by-hit decisions
- Small groups that enjoy learning a system together over multiple sessions

### Skip if
- You need a low-friction family recommendation
- Your group dislikes rules lookups during early plays

### The trade-off
**Its strongest feature (detailed combat feel) is also its biggest barrier.** You get tactical texture, but setup and rules handling can slow momentum at the table.

### Recommendation
Buy only if your group specifically wants this kind of combat texture. Otherwise, First Giants is the safer purchase per pound spent.

## 4) The Lord of the Rings: Fate of the Fellowship (2025)

### Best for
- LOTR fans who value theme immersion
- Groups that enjoy narrative framing and table atmosphere

### Skip if
- Your group is theme-agnostic and mainly chases mechanical efficiency
- You want fast rounds with minimal downtime

### The trade-off
**Theme and atmosphere are the headline; speed and rules-light flow are not.** If your table already has long teach fatigue, this may not fix that.

## UK buying plan: where your money is safer

If you're choosing one purchase this month:
1. **First Giants** (highest table-fit probability)
2. **Legacy of Mars** (only if your group likes heavier optimisation)
3. **Oak Dell** (only if tactical-combat depth is the explicit goal)

If any title is out of stock, check second-hand pricing before overpaying at launch buzz levels.

## Who should skip this whole hotness cycle

Skip all four for now if:
- Your group already has 2+ unplayed heavy strategy boxes
- You mainly play at 2 players and need reliable sub-90-minute sessions
- Your priority is "easy weeknight play" rather than deep system exploration

Sometimes the right move is to wait three weeks, watch post-release play reports, then buy one game confidently instead of panic-buying two.

## Final call

This week, **First Giants** is the practical buy for most UK tables because it balances depth and teachability better than the other hotness movers. **Legacy of Mars** may become the long-term favourite for heavy planners, but it's the riskier blind buy.

If your goal is value, not novelty: buy one, skip one, and wait on the rest.
